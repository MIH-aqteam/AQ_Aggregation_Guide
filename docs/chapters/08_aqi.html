8 European Air Quality Index (AQI)

The European Air Quality Index (AQI) has recently undergone a methodological update to improve its consistency and alignment with international and EU standards. The updated AQI defines concentration bands for each pollutant that correspond to health risk categories based on both the World Health Organization (WHO) 2021 Air Quality Guidelines and the revised Ambient Air Quality Directive (Directive (EU) 2024/2881).

The revision of the AQI methodology and concentration bands is documented in the ETC/HE Report 2024/17: EEA’s revision of the European air quality index bands (EEA, 2025)[30], which provides detailed justification for the updated thresholds and their alignment with the WHO guidelines and EU requirements.

The AQI values are now refreshed hourly, using hourly pollutant concentration data, which enhances its sensitivity to short-term temporal variations. The index remains available at station level, with additional visualization options such as map layers based on modelled concentrations.

Pollutant-specific bands are set according to WHO’s long- and short-term guideline levels and interim targets, with PM2.5 serving as the reference pollutant. Bands for other pollutants are calculated to reflect equivalent relative risks of all-cause mortality associated with short-term exposure. These bands have been further refined to align with EU air quality information and alert thresholds.

This update enhances scientific robustness and communication clarity of the AQI, supporting better public awareness and health protection across EEA member countries.
